Not supposed To say

BLOGS by ARCHITA JAISWAL — SEP 15, 2026

All my life, I have always craved one thing, and that is to be loved. In any form. That too, unconditionally.

Since the day I gained consciousness, I have been taught to be quiet and just follow orders. I was trained to be the Perfect Daughter all my life. Maybe that's why I grew up to be this rebellious as a person.

Not every girl is her father's princess. I toh definitely wasn't.

I was beaten up freaking badly till 11th grade. Literally anything you could imagine as a good thing to beat anybody with, I used to get beaten by all of that. Even now, sometimes.

But the keyword is "sometimes."

The intensity is still the same, but it happens less now.

Guess what still hasn't changed?

Their judgement and criticism.

There's not a single day that passes by without them saying shitty things to me. According to them, doing this is supposed to give me some sort of motivation.

How funny.

Little do they know that it's not motivating at all. It's disheartening and lowers my self-esteem. And because I never got enough right to take my own decisions in life, now making even the silliest of decisions makes me very damn anxious, thinking, what if people judge me?

Apparently, my mom thinks talking and giggling too much on calls with friends for hours is a sign of being a bad daughter.

All my life, my mom has always misunderstood my friendship dynamics, and she's the reason none of my friendships ever lasted.

According to her, being too involved with friends is bad.

And don't even get me started on being a girl in an Indian household.

That too, the eldest.

It's a whole different topic.

But you got the point.

They say, "We gave birth to you. We gave you a house to live in, food to eat, invested in your education."

And my question is—

What were you supposed to do then? Throw me somewhere else?

No, right?

Because that's your DUTY as a parent.

You owe me all of that.

And don't get me wrong, I am truly grateful for it. I know not everyone gets the opportunities I've had, and I will never deny that.

BUT THAT'S ALL.

Where did the love part go?

Where's that?

Because providing me with the basic things a parent is supposed to provide doesn't automatically fill the space where love, understanding, affection, and acceptance are supposed to be.

So yes, thank you for the house.

Thank you for the food.

Thank you for my education.

Thank you for everything you've provided.

But...

Where's the love?

To the world out there, they may seem like very cool and chill parents.

But only I know how narrow and different their mentality actually is.

As their daughter, I know the side of them that the world doesn't.

And honestly, I would never grow up to be like my parents.

Not because I think I'm better than them.

But because I don't want my kids to hate me.
